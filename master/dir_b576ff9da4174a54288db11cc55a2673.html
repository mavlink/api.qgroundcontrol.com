Detailed Description

GStreamer Video Streaming

QGroundControl uses GStreamer for UDP RTP and RTSP video streaming in the Main Flight Display.

This guide covers the GStreamer receiver architecture, platform setup, and diagnostics. See the video module's README for a map of the folders of src/VideoManager and the path of a frame and of a recording through them, CODING_STYLE.md for repository-wide source conventions, test/README.md for the test framework, and tools/README.md for general build and tooling commands.

Source Code Architecture

The backend is split into focused components, by folder:

  • GStreamerVideoBackend, in this folder: the VideoBackend, the app's way into the folders below.
  • Runtime/: initialization, environment, logging, decoder ranks, workarounds and helpers.
  • Source/: the source bins, from a stream's URI to its parsed stream.
  • Pipeline/: the receiver, its worker, sessions and branches.
  • Sink/: QGCQVideoSinkController and the gstqgc plugin (Sink/gstqgc/).
  • HwBuffers/: the zero-copy GPU paths: the HwBuffers facade the other folders call, a folder for each path, and common/, which the paths share.
  • SelfTest/: the boot self-test and the encoded samples it decodes and records (SelfTest/Media/).
  • Platform/: platform shims (IOSPipe2Compat.c) and the Android JNI state GStreamer::mobile keeps for GStreamer's Android plugins (gst_android_jni.c).

The backend-neutral VideoReceiver, VideoBackend and VideoStreamStats are in ../Receiver/. ../QtMultimedia/ holds the other VideoReceiver, QtMultimediaVideoReceiver, which plays streams through QMediaPlayer (FFmpeg): in builds without GStreamer, where VideoSettings::videoBackend picks it, and when GStreamer failed to initialize.

Each receiver plays for one video source, a VideoStreamController (../Streams/), and VideoManager (..) is the QML facade of the sources: the primary stream, the thermal stream, the USB camera (../UVC/), and one for each other stream shown in a tile beside the main picture (VideoTiles.qml and VideoTile.qml in ../Qml/). Those are the other streams the active vehicle's cameras advertise, up to VideoSettings::maxVideoTiles, or to the tiles the row has room for where those are fewer (VideoManager::tileRoom), less the tile of the USB camera that VideoSettings::uvcTileCamera names. A stream at an address (its URI, or the UDP port it is listened for on) that a stream on display already has gets no source: a camera sends such streams one at a time.

  • VideoManager::sources and primaryStream notify. When the vehicle's current stream becomes one that plays in a tile, as after VideoManager::selectSource(), that tile's source becomes the primary stream and the former primary stream takes the tile: both keep their receivers, and neither stream restarts. A stream that plays in no tile is played by the primary stream, whose receiver connects to it.
  • A QML VideoOutput registers with the source it shows (VideoStreamOutput.qml). One that registers as another one leaves takes the source over, so a source moves between the main picture and a tile without its receiver being without an output, which would take the decoder out of its pipeline. A tile's source has an output only while the tile is shown: hidden, its stream is received but not decoded, as the thermal stream is with its view off.
  • On Android and iOS no source has an output while the app is in the background (Qt::ApplicationHidden or Qt::ApplicationSuspended): nothing is decoded there. The streams are still received and recorded, for as long as the system lets them arrive: Android 15 cuts a background app's network after about five seconds, with which the stream times out and a recording ends, to continue in a new part once the app is back.
  • The vehicle's cameras are told which of their streams play in tiles (MavlinkCameraControlInterface::setExtraStreams()). VehicleCameraControl asks the camera to transmit them (MAV_CMD_VIDEO_START_STREAMING) and to stop those that left (MAV_CMD_VIDEO_STOP_STREAMING), and neither stops nor starts a stream that stays on display when the current stream changes.
  • Recordings and photos are of the main picture and its thermal stream, not of the tiles.

The components, in the order of their folders: Pipeline/, Source/, Sink/, Runtime/, SelfTest/, and GStreamerVideoBackend.

Component Role
GstVideoReceiver The VideoReceiver the app uses. Callers state what they want (config, active, video output, recording), which it hands to its GstReceiverWorker as one GstReceiverRequest after each change. It owns the display sink, built from its VideoReceiver::DisplayConfig when an output is set and rebuilt by restart(), which it also calls when its worker reports the stream's graphics device lost. Every 10 s it checks that its worker runs the tasks it posts to it: its requests and, once the worker is through with those, a heartbeat. A worker that has run none for six checks in a row, 60 to 70 s after its last one, is stuck (see Threading): the receiver tells the user once, through errorOccurred, to restart the application, and from then on reports the stream as the worker did last while frames still reach the output, without statistics, and as stopped while none do, so that no frozen picture passes for the live one. It follows the worker again when that runs a task after all.
GstReceiverWorker Runs on the receiver's worker thread and keeps one PipelineSession in line with what the receiver was asked for, reconnecting after a stream failure as its StreamSupervisor (src/VideoManager/Receiver, shared with the Qt Multimedia receiver) paces it, and giving up (State::Stopped plus errorOccurred) when reconnecting is off or can't help (unsupported URI, missing plugin, credentials an RTSP server refuses). A recording continues across reconnects, from hour to hour, and when its RecordingBranch asks for another file, in the parts its RecordingPlan (src/VideoManager/Receiver) names. Acts on what differs from the previous request, and reports back through queued signals: its whole GstReceiverStatus (state, decoding, decoder, recording, stats) after each change, and the recording and error events. Each task the receiver posts to it, a request or its heartbeat, ends with taskDone, which tells the receiver that the worker still gets to them.
PipelineSession One pipeline (source → tee → the branches below) and everything about it: elements, bus, probes. Lives on the worker; replaces a decoder that posts an error by itself (a stalled one when the worker's watchdog asks) and reports failures of the stream to the worker.
PipelineStatsProbe What the session measures for the stream statistics apart from its branches: the bytes and keyframe timestamps the tee's probe notes, and, when sampled, the RTP counters of the source's jitter buffers with the packets their drop-msg messages tell they dropped (those of its GstRtpPacketProbe when it has none), and the latency and codec the pipeline reports.
GstBusPump The session's bus: a sync handler on the posting thread for what can't wait, the other messages popped on the worker when the bus's poll fd wakes it, a timed pump for a recording's teardown, and a detach that waits out in-flight sync calls so nothing reaches the session afterwards.
DecodingBranch Display branch behind the tee: inserts decodebin3 once the source streams, asks upstream for a keyframe, and reports decoding, the decoder name, and a decoder that keeps failing or can't be inserted, which the worker treats as a failure of the stream. A hardware decoder whose replacement brought no frame either is taken out of autoplug (GStreamer::demoteFailedDecoder()), so the restart that follows plugs the decoder ranked after it, unless the graphics device was reported removed, when no decoder is at fault. A hardware decoder plugged before the pipeline got the graphics device Qt renders with is replaced once it has (see GstContextBridgeBase below), since it would keep decoding on a device of its own. Behind a source that isn't live (tcp://) it sets the decoder's low-latency properties (libav's thread-type, nvcodec's max-display-delay, the stateless H.264 decoders' compliance), which a decoder otherwise picks only for a live source. Its 2-buffer queue drops the oldest frame rather than hold up the tee, once the decoder has fallen behind by more than decodebin3 buffers ahead of it: decodebin3's multiqueue holds the stream by time, with no limit on buffers, up to its min-interleave-time (250 ms by default, the decoder's latency plus 100 ms once the decoder reports one, and no less than the pipeline's latency in GStreamer 1.24 and the later 1.22 releases). After a drop it also drops delta frames until the next keyframe, so the picture freezes briefly instead of smearing. It waits 2 s at most for that keyframe, which it requests upstream, and logs when none came: not every source passes the request on to the sender.
RecordingBranch Recording branch behind the tee: a parser ! muxer ! filesink bin for each file the session is asked to record, started at a keyframe (requested upstream) and, for MP4/MOV, muxed so a crash leaves a playable file; an MKV is synced to the disk every 5 s, as qtmux has its header updates synced, since storage that caches writes (an Android SD card, a network share) reports a full disk only to a sync. A file is synced once more after its muxer's last write, before it is reported closed, so that its end survives a power cut and a disk that was full for it is reported: qtmux asks for that sync with the last thing it writes, and an MKV gets it from a buffer ahead of the EOS that holds an empty memory, since filesink writes out what it holds back, and then syncs, only for a buffer that has a memory. The sync runs on the thread that brings the recorder its EOS, within the 3 s a file has to finalize. The stream's first keyframe lets it into the recorder, and the first one the recorder's parser passes on, which comes with its parameter sets, starts the file at time zero; the recording fails when none does within 5 s. After a reconnect, after each hour, when an MP4/MOV part has less than a minute of the header space it reserved left (the muxer's reserved-duration-remaining, read every second), and when the stream's caps change (a new resolution or profile: matroskamux refuses other caps in a file it has begun, and qtmux takes only some, so each file holds one format), the recording goes on in <base>_part<N>.<ext>; a part that takes over from a running one asks upstream for a keyframe and starts with the one the running part ends before, or with the new caps, which are held at the valve until then, so no frame is lost between them. Its queue holds 64 MB of backlog for slow storage, then ends the recording rather than stall the display. A recorder's error ends the recording too, reported with GStreamer's text for it, or as a full storage when filesink reports one: by the error's code for a failed write, and by errno's text in the detail for a failed fsync.
TeeBranchHead The tee ! queue ! valve head both branches start with: built closed and marked as its branch, opened from a keyframe requested upstream, closed before the branch takes out what follows it. absorbFlowErrors() has the part that follows it (decodebin3, the recorder bin) post a push that fails as its own ERROR and report success, so an element that fails without posting one (a muxer or decoder refusing a format change) fails its branch, not the tee.
BranchContext What PipelineSession lends its branches: the pipeline, the tee, the watchdog, the dot dumper and a way to queue work on the worker. Also the branch marks (GStreamer::markBranch(), GStreamer::branchOf()) that blame a bus message on the branch it came from.
StreamWatchdog Notices stalled source or decoder output on a monotonic clock. Before the first frame it allows rtspsrc its UDP→TCP fallback, and a UDP source any time at all: it keeps listening until a stream arrives. What the worker does about a stall, a new decoder or a reconnect, doesn't end a streaming thread that never returns: the worker waits for it, which the receiver's check of its worker reports.
PipelineDotDumper Writes the pipeline's .dot graphs; see Pipeline Observability.
GstSourceFactory Constructs the source element from the stream URI, for the transport its scheme selects (VideoStreamUri in src/VideoManager/Receiver: rtsp*, udp, udp265, mpegts, tcp), and applies RTP jitter-buffer policy for application/x-rtp sources. RTP over UDP gets its codec's depayloader and parser, and, without a jitter buffer (low-latency mode), a GstRtpPacketProbe on udpsrc to count its packets; an RTSP stream of H.264 or H.265 gets the same depayloader and parser, in place of parsebin, once the SDP has told its encoding, so that a frame leaves the bin when its last packet arrives rather than when the next frame starts; other RTSP streams and MPEG-TS are left to parsebin. An RTSP stream that goes on under another SSRC or payload type, as when a camera starts its encoder over, is followed onto the pad rtspsrc adds for it.
GstRtpPacketProbe GStreamer::countRtpPackets(): a probe that counts the RTP packets a pad pushes, for a source that has no jitter buffer to count them. On the streaming thread it reads the fixed 12-byte header of each packet, in buffers and buffer lists, into an RtpPacketCounter, and hands the totals over in atomics for GStreamer::rtpPacketCounts().
RtpPacketCounter The packets received and lost in one RTP stream, from their sequence numbers as RFC 3550 counts them (wraparound, late and duplicate packets, a sender that starts over), and its interarrival jitter. No GStreamer types.
GstRtspSource The rtspsrc the source factory builds: the RTSP URI check, transports, timeouts, latency, the UDP receive buffer that udp:// streams get too, retransmission timing, video-only stream selection, an SDP that lets keyframe requests through (a=rtcp-fb: nack pli), and a stop that sends TEARDOWN, with a second to be answered, and no PAUSE ahead of it.
GstH26xParameterSetGate H.264/H.265 setup for the depayloaders and parsers the source factory builds or parsebin plugs: parameter sets repeated at every IDR, keyframe requests after loss, and output held until a full parameter set arrives.
QGCQVideoSinkController GUI-thread companion of the display sink; see `gstqgc`.
GStreamer The facade QGC calls, through VideoBackend where it must also build without GStreamer: initialization (initialize(), with completeInit() checking the plugin features) and the display sink (createVideoSink() configured from the receiver's VideoReceiver::DisplayConfig, bound to a QVideoSink by setupQVideoSinkElement()).
GStreamerEnvironment Process-wide environment setup (plugin/scanner/helper/GIO paths, private registry, mobile CA file) run before gst_init(), with each desktop install's layout (bundleLayout(): macOS framework or flat SDK, Windows, Linux) a function of the app directory that tests check on any host. See Runtime Environment Setup.
GStreamerDecoderRanks Video decoder classification (hardware or software, and the forceVideoDecoder family) and DecoderRankApplier, which applies the setting's rank plan to the registry from the decoders' original ranks (ScopedDecoderOption applies one for a scope), applies it again once the scene graph publishes the device Qt renders with, as the plan depends on that device's API, its adapter and what it opens (reapply()), and takes a hardware decoder that keeps failing out of autoplug until the setting is applied again (demoteFailedDecoder()); also the decoder families offered and the rank dump logged under Video.GStreamer.DecoderRanks.
GStreamerDecoderRankPlan planRanks(): the rank changes a forceVideoDecoder option makes to the installed decoders, with the platform rules (zero-copy va/v4l2sl over vaapi on Linux, on Windows the D3D decoders Qt's renderer samples zero-copy on the adapter it renders on: d3d12 under Direct3D 12, and under Direct3D 11 where its device opens their frames, else d3d11) as RankTraits, without the registry. The software and hardware options only take the other class out of autoplug: a decoder keeps the rank GStreamer gives it, so one it ranks NONE is plugged only when its family is forced. The Linux rule likewise raises the va decoders GStreamer autoplugs and leaves those it ranks NONE (vajpegdec, vah266dec), except where it ranks them all NONE, as it did before 1.24. Where the user asked for software rendering (--swrast, the GPU Safe Mode shortcut), the default option ranks every hardware decoder below the software decoders, so video keeps off the GPU as well; an option chosen in the video settings stays as it is.
GStreamerHelpers Small utilities: keyframe requests, events sent without blocking, holding a new pad's data until it is linked, and taking a child element out of a running bin.
GStreamerLibavWorkaround Has avdec_h265 decode on one thread with FFmpeg 7.1.0 (libavcodec 61.19.100, which GStreamer 1.28 packages for Android, iOS, macOS and Windows), whose slice threads deadlock on an HEVC picture with more wavefront rows than threads and take the streaming thread with them; DecodingBranch applies it to each decoder decodebin3 plugs. The libavcodec version comes from its pkg-config file at configure time, and on iOS, whose SDK has none, from its version headers.
GStreamerLogging Routes GStreamer's log output through QGC's logging, unless GStreamer's own output was requested, and applies the persisted debug level. See GStreamer Logging.
GStreamerUdpSrcWorkaround Serializes udpsrc's unlock()/unlock_stop() on gst-plugins-good before 1.24.10, whose race corrupts the heap when a UDP stream stops right after it starts; completeInit() applies it.
GstScoped RAII owners for transfer-full GStreamer returns so refs can't leak on early return, makeElement() (never floating), PadProbe and ScopedSignal.
GStreamerSelfTest GStreamer::SelfTest::run(), which the boot test runs to prove the packaged runtime decodes and records. See Boot self-test.
GStreamerSelfTestMedia The self-test's media pipelines: decoding the embedded samples and recording and reading back MP4 and MKV.
GStreamerVideoBackend The VideoBackend implementation: the only way the app reaches GStreamer's process lifecycle, which it keeps as states (unprepared, prepared, initialized, failed): the environment first, initialization once (a failed or repeated one isn't retried, as gst_init() can't be undone), the self-test only once initialized. Also GStreamer's tuning (decoder priorities, debug level, Android JNI state). Settings reach the backend as values: VideoReceiver::Config, VideoReceiver::DisplayConfig and the persisted debug level. Once GStreamer has failed to initialize, its receivers are QtMultimediaVideoReceivers, and VideoManager shows its initFailureMessage().

MockLink's MockVideoStreamServer (src/Comms/MockLink, Debug builds only) serves a synthetic videotestsrc stream (RTP H.264 or H.265 over UDP, RTSP, MPEG-TS over UDP or TCP) that MockLink advertises, so the receiver runs without a vehicle; builds without GStreamer compile a null server instead.

Threading

  • GUI thread: GstVideoReceiver's API and every signal it emits, the display sink's QGCQVideoSinkController, and the video size, read from each frame the output's QVideoSink shows: its viewport (decoder padding and crop left out), turned as Qt presents it.
  • Worker thread (one per receiver, running its GstReceiverWorker): the reconnect logic, every change to the pipeline, and its bus. A sync handler only does what can't wait: answers NEED_CONTEXT, selects the video streams, and closes the valve in front of a branch that posted an ERROR, so the error stays out of the tee (a failed push without an ERROR gets one posted at the branch's entrance, TeeBranchHead::absorbFlowErrors()). Everything else is popped on the worker, which a QSocketNotifier (QWinEventNotifier on Windows) on the bus's poll fd wakes up, as Qt's QGstBusObserver does.
  • Streaming threads: probes and element signals only note what happened and queue the rest on their PipelineSession, which drops it once the pipeline is gone.
  • Teardown is bounded: an open recording drains through an EOS sent from GStreamer's thread pool, the worker waits for it at most 3 s and then flushes the recorder, and the receiver's destructor waits at most 5 s for the worker, leaving one stuck in the kernel (a hung network mount) to finish on its own. The worker reaches the receiver only through signals queued to it, which Qt drops once the receiver is gone.
  • A stuck worker: stopping an element waits for its streaming thread, so a thread that never returns (a decoder deadlocked in its library, a recorder stuck in the kernel) holds the worker for good, and with it the pipeline's state lock. No request is served any more, and the pipeline can neither be stopped nor replaced: a second one would not get the UDP port, or the decoder, that the first still holds. The receiver's check of its worker, a single-shot QTimer on the GUI thread that each check starts again, notices it: GstVideoReceiver above says what the user is told. Its interval, 10 s, is longer than the worker's longest waits in a row (3 s for a recording to finalize, 1 s for an RTSP TEARDOWN, 3 s for a TCP connect), and it takes six checks in a row, each a full interval after the one before, at which the worker has run none of its tasks since the check before: the name lookup for a tcp:// or udp:// host runs on the worker too, and can take half a minute to fail. So a worker that is busy, or behind with a row of slow requests, isn't reported, nor one that a suspended machine or a stopped process gave no time. The timer stops once the worker of a receiver that wants no stream is through with what it was posted.

gstqgc — custom GStreamer plugin

A QGC-owned plugin (gstqgc/) bridging the pipeline into Qt:

  • qgcqvideosink — a GstVideoSink that pushes decoded frames into a Qt QVideoSink. Delivery (GstQgcFrameDelivery) keeps only the latest frame, so a stalled GUI thread never holds more than one undelivered frame (or the decoder buffer behind it). A frame is as large as its buffer's GstVideoMeta, which a decoder pads beyond the caps (1088 rows for 1080p) or leaves uncropped, and its viewport is the picture in it: the buffer's GstVideoCropMeta, else the caps' size at the frame's origin (frameGeometry() in GstQgcFrameMap). The CPU, DMABuf and GLMemory paths show the frame at that size. A GstVideoAffineTransformationMeta narrows the viewport and flips the frame: Android's amcvideodec attaches one with its SurfaceTexture's crop and orientation, which glcolorconvert hands on unapplied. Only scales, translations and flips are applied, and the sink asks no element for the meta. glcolorconvert squeezes the decoder's padded buffer into a texture of the caps' size, so that viewport is smaller than the picture (1072 rows for 1080p): the sink's picture-width and picture-height properties give the picture's own size, which the receiver reports as the video size. Frames carry the rotation and mirroring of the stream's image-orientation tag, as a camera mounted sideways sends it. The VideoOutput showing them adds the rotation a MAVLink camera reports for the stream (VideoStreamController::rotation) on top, as its orientation. Its construct-only gpu-zerocopy property (passed down by the bin) enables zero-copy import of GPU memory, and its frame-stats property reports that sink's own frame counts per delivery path, GPU fallbacks, dropped tiled frames (GstQgc::formatSinkStats in GstQgcSinkStats.h formats them) and delivered frames a newer one replaced before the QVideoSink took them (superseded-frames), all counted by its GstQgcSinkCounters; GstBaseSink's standard stats property is left as is. Its GstQgcPathSession keeps what holds for one caps epoch (the GPU path resolved, one demotion and one tiled-drop warning per epoch) and fails the stream after 120 frames in a row fail to map.

  • Sink caps — qgcqvideosink answers caps queries itself (GstQgc::buildSinkCaps), with the formats Qt renders (kVideoFormatTable):

    • System memory always.
    • Every entry asks for pixel-aspect-ratio=1/1 unless the bin's disable-par is set. The converter scales system memory to square pixels; nothing in the bin scales GPU memory (d3d12convert aside), so a decoder whose pixels aren't square negotiates system memory.
    • With gpu-zerocopy, ahead of it, only the GPU memory the renderer Qt Quick uses can import. Under OpenGL: DMA_DRM and DMABuf on EGL while the DMABuf import works (see DMABuf zero-copy diagnostics), AHardwareBuffer on EGL (Android), and GLMemory, which reaches Qt through the GL share group, on EGL or GLX. Under Direct3D11: D3D11Memory, and ahead of it NV12 and P010 D3D12Memory once Qt's device is known to open it (see D3D12 frames under Direct3D 11). Under Direct3D12, the d3d12convert variant (below). Under Vulkan: DMA_DRM in the layouts Qt's device imports (see DMABuf under Vulkan). Metal and OpenGL on WGL or CGL get system memory only. vtdec's IOSurface-backed frames arrive as system memory.
    • GLMemory always carries texture-target=2D, the only target the GL path samples. It also makes glcolorconvert convert amcvideodec's external-OES textures, which Qt would otherwise sample black.
    • The glupload variant (see GPU bin selection) takes GLMemory only.
    • The d3d12convert variant, the bin Qt's Direct3D12 renderer always gets, takes RGBA or BGRA D3D12Memory only: its d3d12upload ! d3d12convert uploads software-decoded frames and converts the d3d12 decoders' NV12/P010 on the GPU, because Qt's D3D12 QRhi creates no per-plane views and can't sample a planar resource's chroma (the D3D12 import sends any multi-plane frame to the CPU path). A D3D12 device is a per-adapter singleton, so the device GStreamer opens on Qt's adapter is Qt's own, and the decoders are ranked to match the renderer.

    The renderer is the backend of the scene graph's QRhi (QGCRhiCapture), or, until the scene graph runs, the graphics API Qt Quick was asked for. When it changes mid-stream, or the DMABuf import starts or stops working, the sink asks upstream to renegotiate.

  • qgcvideosinkbin — converter ! qgcqvideosink, with glupload ! glcolorconvert in between for the glupload variant and d3d12upload ! d3d12convert for the d3d12convert variant. The converter is the conversion-element override, else imxvideoconvert_g2d or nvvidconv when present, else videoconvertscale (videoconvert ! videoscale before GStreamer 1.22). It scales non-square pixels to square ones, converts formats the sink doesn't take (such as a software decoder's Y444), and passes GPU memory through. When Qt renders with Direct3D 11, an override that names a filter of GStreamer's d3d11 plugin other than d3d11upload and d3d11download (such as d3d11convert or d3d11deinterlace) is ignored: a pipeline's d3d11 elements share the device Qt renders with, and such a filter draws on its immediate context, whose state Qt caches. Decoders and the two elements named only copy there.

    The bin doesn't deinterlace: an interlaced stream (576i or 1080i, as analogue-to-IP encoders send) is shown woven, both fields in each frame, so motion combs. Upstream's playsink plugs gst-plugins-good's deinterlace for that. Here it would reach only software-decoded frames, the bundles don't ship its plugin (the gstreamer.plugins lists of .github/build-config.json), and on an interlaced stream it holds back a field or more (20 ms at 50 fields a second with its default method) and doubles the frame rate. A stream in interlace-mode=alternate doesn't negotiate at all, as the sink doesn't offer the format:Interlaced caps feature; decoders that can send both send interleaved frames.

  • Allocation proposal — the sink asks the decoder for at least five buffers (kMaxPinnedFrames + 1), covering the frames Qt may still hold, also of a producer that asks for no pool, and keeps the video and crop metas it reads. D3D11Memory and D3D12Memory get no crop meta: the decoders then crop a picture that starts off their texture's origin themselves, where the D3D paths would copy it from the origin. It asks for GL sync metas as upstream's Qt sink does, which has glcolorconvert end each frame with a sync point. The GL path leaves a frame's sync meta alone, since other consumers share the buffer: it sets a sync point of its own, in a buffer its texture bundles hand on, and waits for it in Qt's context. QGC_GST_PROPOSE_ALLOCATION_MIN_BUFFERS overrides the count, as Qt's QT_GSTREAMER_PROPOSE_ALLOCATION_MIN_BUFFERS does, e.g. to save CMA memory with 4K decoders on i.MX or Raspberry Pi.
  • QGCQVideoSinkController — GUI-thread companion that binds the sink to a QVideoSink, lets it deliver only while the output's window is visible, and clears the frame that QVideoSink displays when decoding stops.

HwBuffers — GPU paths

Per-platform import of decoded GPU frames without a copy. Most paths wrap their native textures in GstHwFrameTexturesBase : QVideoFrameTextures; the DMABuf path hands its planes to Qt Multimedia's DMABuf importer (dmabuf/GstQtDmaBufImport, the only code using that private Qt API), or, when Qt Quick renders with Vulkan, imports them as VkImages itself (dmabuf/GstDmaBufVulkanImport):

  • Platforms: dmabuf (Linux/VA-API), gl (Linux, Android), vulkan, d3d (D3D11/D3D12), cuda, apple (IOSurface), android (AHardwareBuffer).
  • HwPathDescriptor: each backend describes its path once (memory match, buffer construction, cache reset, pool proposal, context bridge). GstHwPathRegistry lists the compiled ones in a constant array. The factory selects the first path whose descriptor matches a frame's memory, and the facade loops over the list for bus messages, context queries, restarts and resets.
  • GstContextBridgeBase: GL, D3D11 and D3D12 bridges hand Qt's GPU device to GStreamer elements through NEED_CONTEXT and context queries (the Vulkan bridge can't: see the opt-in paths below). Under a Direct3D 11 renderer the D3D12 bridge hands out a device of GStreamer's own on Qt's adapter instead. Qt's is the main window's device: a VideoOutput popped out into its own window renders on that window's device, so on Direct3D11 its frames take the CPU path. So do Direct3D frames a decoder allocated on another device before the bridge was primed, and GLMemory frames from a GL context outside Qt's share group, which gst-gl creates for itself when no element was handed the GL bridge's. The GL bridge limits gst-gl to the OpenGL API of Qt's context, desktop OpenGL or OpenGL ES: gst-gl would otherwise create OpenGL ES contexts wherever EGL offers them, and EGL shares only within one API. A bridge sets the context it answers a NEED_CONTEXT with on the pipeline of the element that asked, and the pipeline hands it to the elements it is given later. An element that asked before Qt's device was known made a device of its own, which its pipeline would hand on instead without asking again: when the scene graph publishes Qt's device, the bridges give those pipelines their contexts. Elements built from then on use Qt's device; the one that made its own keeps it until it is rebuilt. So each bridge also posts a qgc-graphics-device-served element message on such a pipeline, and the session replaces a hardware decoder plugged by then (DecodingBranch): a Direct3D 11 decoder left on its own device would have every frame copied through the CPU for as long as the stream ran. When GStreamer reports the Direct3D device a bridge shares removed (its devices' device-removed-reason, from 1.26: a driver reset or update), the bridges and the paths' caches are dropped as they are when Qt tears its scene graph down, and every pipeline a bridge gave its device gets a qgc-graphics-device-lost element message. Its receiver then restarts the stream with a new video sink, so that nothing holds the removed device: Direct3D 12 creates no new device on an adapter while the removed one is referenced, which would keep Qt from recovering. The new pipeline gets the device Qt renders with by then, or the one it publishes later. Until a bridge has a device again, a decoder that fails is not taken out of autoplug: none works on a removed device.
  • D3D11 decoder padding: decoders pad their textures to their alignment (16 rows for H.264, 128 for HEVC/AV1) without crop meta. The picture is copied out on the render thread, as a texture-array slice is, so frames keep the picture's size and a CPU layout for that fallback and for screenshots.
  • D3D12 texture arrays: no slice of a texture array reaches the sink. gst-d3d12's pools hold single textures, and its decoders copy a frame out of a reference-only array before they hand it on. QRhi can't sample one slice, so the path takes none: such a frame would go to the CPU.
  • D3D12 frames under Direct3D 11: a Direct3D 11 QRhi samples a D3D12Memory frame without GStreamer using Qt's device. The frame's resource opens as a texture of that device (gst_d3d12_memory_get_d3d11_texture, which asks for a shared heap and a render-target binding: the pool the sink proposes has both), the render thread copies the picture out of it as it does for a d3d11 decoder, and signals a fence the two devices share; the frame's buffer goes back to its pool once Direct3D 11 has read it. The sink offers that memory only where Qt's device reports the sharing gst-d3d11 asks of a device for its own interop (D3D11_SHARED_RESOURCE_TIER_2, extended resource sharing and shared NV12 textures). WARP reports it and then opens NV12 without its chroma plane; no decoder shares its adapter, so nothing sends it such a frame. The NVIDIA decoders take this path, as they output D3D12Memory when a sink takes it. So do the d3d12 decoders, which are the default under Direct3D 11 where Qt's device reports that sharing, as they are in GStreamer's own ranking: they keep GStreamer off the device Qt draws with, and a video popped out into its own window imports their frames on that window's device too. The d3d11 decoders stay ranked behind them, and are the default where Qt's device lacks the sharing; GST_PLUGIN_FEATURE_RANK (d3d11h264dec:MAX) selects one anyway. A decoder on another adapter than Qt's goes to the CPU path, as with the D3D11 path, and ranks below the software decoders once Qt's adapter is known.
  • D3D12 resource states: the import hands every resource to QRhi as D3D12_RESOURCE_STATE_COMMON. Qt transitions a texture it samples to a shader-resource state and never back (QD3D12ResourceBarrierGenerator, Qt 6.12), while the converter's pool recycles its resources. The D3D12 debug layer reports no state error for it (Qt 6.12.0, GStreamer 1.28.8, Intel Arc, 875 frames). To repeat the run, set GST_ENABLE_D3D12_DEBUG=1 along with QSG_RHI_DEBUG_LAYER=1: the layer can't be turned on for a device GStreamer already created, and Qt then falls back to WARP.
  • GstHwPathTelemetry tracks process-wide render-time counters (map failures, reuse hits, sync waits) for fallback diagnostics. A receiver's GPU path live and GPU path stats debug lines pair its own sink's frame-stats with them, both counted from when its session took the sink. Reading never resets the counters: each reader reports the change since its own snapshot, so receivers don't take each other's counts.
  • CpuVideoFramePool recycles CPU-backed QVideoFrame storage (WebRTC-style slab) for the software-fallback path, avoiding per-frame allocation.
  • Build options: HwBuffers/CMakeLists.txt probes one table row per path; the QGC_GST_ENABLE_<PATH> switches that compile a path in or out are listed in cmake/README.md. Windows builds leave out the GLMemory path: Qt 6's OpenGL there is WGL, which gst-gl can't share. Linux builds with gst-gl and DMABuf compile both GPU bins; see GPU bin selection.
  • Opt-in paths: the CUDA, AHardwareBuffer and Vulkan paths are compiled only with QGC_GST_ENABLE_<PATH>=ON, because no stream reaches them: no bin advertises CUDAMemory or VulkanImage caps, and Android's glupload bin offers only GLMemory. Configure lists the compiled paths that the sink can't reach at runtime. None of them is a zero-copy path that works today:
    • Vulkan (vulkan/, gst-vulkan's VulkanImage memory): gst-vulkan creates its own VkDevice and has no way to wrap Qt's (gst_vulkan_device_new() takes a physical device; so in 1.24, 1.28 and on main), and QRhi can't sample an image of another device. GstVulkanVideoBuffer therefore imports no frame, and the Vulkan bridge only puts GStreamer's device on the physical device Qt renders on. A VulkanImage frame that did reach the sink would not be shown: gst-vulkan's pools allocate device-local memory, which it doesn't map on the CPU either. In a build with the path, GstHwBuffersVulkanReadbackTest checks the bridge's device and the refused import. gst-vulkan looks for a device on its pads only, so an element that isn't linked to the sink when it opens its device gets the bridge's only from gst-vulkan 1.26, through the instance's create-device signal; before, it opens the first physical device.
    • CUDA (cuda/, gst-cuda's CUDAMemory): never run on NVIDIA hardware, only compiled, against a stub cuda.h. The path wraps the fd of gst_cuda_memory_export() as a dma-buf for the DMABuf path. gst-cuda exports only memory of a GST_CUDA_MEMORY_ALLOC_MMAP pool, which nothing in QGC proposes to a decoder, so a decoder's frames aren't admitted and take the CPU path. And the fd is CUDA's shareable handle (cuMemExportToShareableHandle), which the desktop driver's EGL is reported to refuse as a dma-buf: the path asks the kernel whether it is one, and turns itself off for the process when it isn't rather than fail every import. The route to try on NVIDIA hardware is cudadownload to GLMemory (a copy on the GPU, where gst-cuda is built with OpenGL support) into the glupload bin.
    • AHardwareBuffer (android/): never compiled for Android, let alone run. It is written against <gst/android/gstandroid.h> (gst_ahardware_buffer_memory_get_buffer()), which no GStreamer release has, so QGC_GST_ENABLE_AHARDWAREBUFFER=ON finds nothing to build against in the SDK QGC downloads. GStreamer 1.30 brings an AHardwareBuffer memory of its own, which this path can't take as it is: another header (<gst/allocators/gstahardwarebuffer.h>), another getter (gst_ahardware_buffer_memory_peek_buffer()) and caps with format=AHARDWARE_BUFFER and an ahb-format field, where the sink offers the path NV12-style formats. It also gives glupload an uploader for that memory, so the glupload bin may need no path of QGC's for it. Its source is only syntax-checked on Linux against a stub of that header. Its frames are external-OES textures with no CPU layout Qt could show instead, so the path takes a frame only while it can import it: not under a renderer other than OpenGL, nor after the EGL display lacked EGL_ANDROID_image_native_buffer or the driver refused frame after frame. Android's zero-copy path is GLMemory through the glupload bin.

Stream statistics

VideoReceiver::stats (VideoStreamStats, a QML value type that VideoStreamController forwards) tells how healthy a stream is. The Fly View toolbar's video indicator shows those of VideoManager::measuredSource, and turns orange while VideoManager::degraded tells that a source falls short (VideoStreamStats::degraded()). While the stream is Streaming, GstReceiverWorker samples its PipelineSession on each watchdog tick (once a second) through VideoStreamStatsSampler (../Receiver/VideoStreamStatsSampler.h, shared with the Qt Multimedia receiver): its VideoStreamStatsMeter, a pure class with an injectable clock, turns the cumulative counters into rates, and it adds the session's fields below. The first sample is the rates' baseline, so stats are valid from the second one on. The worker hands them to the receiver like its other reports, so statsChanged is emitted on the GUI thread, and sends invalid stats when its session stops: on a stop, a reconnect or a failure. The streaming threads only add to atomic counters, with no lock and no allocation per frame or packet; everything else is read on the worker.

Field Measured from
fps Frames the display sink delivered to the QVideoSink (its frame-stats counts per path) less those it superseded, per second over the last VideoStreamStatsMeter::kRateWindowIntervals samples.
bitrateKbps The compressed elementary stream reaching the tee, after the depayloader or demuxer and the parser, counted by the tee's buffer probe, over the same window as fps.
framesDropped, droppedFps Since the stream started, and per second over the last interval: buffers the display queue leaked (its overruns, counted once the buffer after them comes out marked DISCONT, which tells that the queue did leak), delta frames its keyframe gate dropped, frames the display sink dropped because they arrived too late for their time (dropped in its base sink's stats; none in low-latency mode, whose sink doesn't sync), and superseded frames.
packetsLost, lossPercent, jitterMs The stats of every rtpjitterbuffer in the source bin, rtspsrc's internal ones included: num-lost since the stream started, num-lost against num-pushed over the last second, and avg-jitter. Lost are also the packets a jitter buffer drops on arrival to stay within its latency (drop-on-latency), as after a burst: they are in none of its stats, so the source factory has it post a drop-msg element message for each (post-drop-messages), whose num-drop-on-latency PipelineStatsProbe adds up from the bus. UDP RTP in low-latency mode has no jitter buffer: the source bin's GstRtpPacketProbe counts the packets udpsrc receives instead (below). -1 for MPEG-TS, which carries no RTP.
latencyMs The minimum latency of a latency query on the pipeline, as logged when it reaches PLAYING. 0 in low-latency mode, whose display sink doesn't sync.
decoder, hardwareDecoder The decoder factory decodebin3 plugged last, and GStreamer::isHardwareDecoderFactory(). Empty and false while no decoder is inserted: without a video output, and while a decoder is being replaced.
gpuPath The delivery path most frames took since the previous sample, as GstHw::toString() names it: CPU, DMABuf, GL, D3D11, D3D12, IOSurface, AHWBuf or Vulkan. Empty for a display sink other than qgcvideosinkbin.
codec The caps on the tee: H.264, H.265, AV1, VP8, VP9, MJPEG, MPEG-2, ...
packetsLate, packetsDuplicate, rtxRequested, rtxRecovered The same jitter buffers' num-late, num-duplicates, rtx-count and rtx-success-count, since the stream started. -1 without a jitter buffer, which alone tells them: MPEG-TS, and UDP RTP in low-latency mode.
keyframeIntervalMs The tee's buffer probe (PipelineStatsProbe): the average time between the timestamps (PTS, else DTS) of the last five buffers without DELTA_UNIT, so the arrival times a stream without a jitter buffer is stamped with don't swing it. -1 until two keyframes arrived, so a stream without keyframes, such as one with intra refresh, never has one.
gpuFallbacks The display sink's gpu-fallbacks count since the stream started, the sinks a decoder restart replaced included.
decoderRestarts Decoders DecodingBranch::restartDecoder() replaced since the stream started.
uptimeMs, reconnects The worker's StreamSupervisor: the time since the session reported its first frame, and the sessions that streamed since the stream was started or restarted, less one.
recordingMs, recordingBytes The worker's RecordingPlan, while recording: the time since the first part got its first frame, and the sizes of the parts' files. -1 otherwise.

Without a jitter buffer, RtpPacketCounter counts as RFC 3550 (appendix A.1) has a receiver do. Lost packets are the sequence numbers missing between the first packet and the highest one, across the 16-bit wraparounds, so a loss shows with the next packet that arrives, where a jitter buffer first waits out its latency. A packet that arrives late, fewer than 1024 packets behind, is no longer lost, and a duplicate counts once. PipelineStatsProbe gives the meter a lost count that only grows, since the meter takes a counter that shrank for one that restarted: a late packet that a sample counted as lost stays in the total until the next loss makes up for it. A sender that starts over begins a new sequence: with another SSRC, or with sequence numbers 1024 or more behind the highest one or too far ahead of it to be loss, and either way once a second packet goes on from the first, so one stray packet changes nothing. Too far ahead is what the sender sends in ten seconds at the packet rate measured from the RTP timestamps, as rtpbin scales its max-dropout-time, and 3000 packets before a second of the stream has measured it. The totals go on, without the packets lost in between. The jitter is the interarrival jitter of appendix A.8 from the RTP timestamps (90 kHz) and the arrival times udpsrc stamps its buffers with, which is how rtpjitterbuffer measures its avg-jitter.

VideoStreamStatsMeterTest covers the rate math on a fake clock and the sampler's session fields, GstVideoReceiverComponentsTest the packet counter and its probe, GstVideoReceiverStatsTest the stats of real streams.

Build Configuration

  • Enable/disable: Set the QGC_ENABLE_GST_VIDEOSTREAMING CMake option to ON/OFF. With OFF, streams play through QtMultimediaVideoReceiver and can't be recorded.
  • Versions, checksums and plugins: gstreamer.version.<platform>, gstreamer.checksums, gstreamer.plugins and the iOS CA-bundle pin live in .github/build-config.json, read through qgc_config_read (cmake/modules/BuildConfigReader.cmake). The download URLs (upstream, with the QGC S3 mirror as fallback) are templated from the version in cmake/GStreamer/Packages.cmake; a package is verified against its checksum whichever host serves it. mirror-gstreamer.yml fills the mirror whenever a master push changes build-config.json. Linux uses the system GStreamer and treats gstreamer.version.minimum as its floor; every other platform's official SDK must be at least gstreamer.version.bundled_minimum, which configure, tools/validate_configs.py, the S3 mirror and GStreamerTest all read.
  • Configure options:
    • GStreamer_ROOT_DIR: the SDK to use (on Linux, a prefix whose lib*/pkgconfig or lib/<multiarch>/pkgconfig holds gstreamer-1.0.pc). Configure never writes it; the SDK it selected is recorded as QGC_GST_RESOLVED_ROOT in CMakeCache.txt. pkg-config prints paths unquoted, so an SDK path with spaces fails configure, except on iOS, which reads its SDK without pkg-config. An installed Windows SDK in one (C:/Program Files/...) is skipped with a warning in favor of a download. SDKs are downloaded to CPM_SOURCE_CACHE, which defaults to .cache/CPM in the source tree: when its path has spaces, a Windows host downloads through its 8.3 short path, and configure fails on other hosts, or where the volume has no short names, until CPM_SOURCE_CACHE is set to a path without spaces.
    • QGC_GST_VERSION: overrides the build-config version (the minimum on Linux, the SDK to download elsewhere), as <major>.<minor>.<micro>.
    • QGC_GST_BUNDLE_PLUGINS (Linux, default ON): installs and AppImages bundle the system's plugins, plugin scanner and GIO modules (see Linux), and the app points GStreamer at them. Distribution packages set it OFF: the install then carries nothing of GStreamer, the app uses the system's plugins as they are, and the package must depend on the distribution's plugin packages. The Arch package then adds gst-plugins-good, gst-plugins-bad-libs and gst-libav; the DEB and RPM dependency scans do not see plugins, so their packagers list them. OFF requires QGC_CREATE_APPIMAGE=OFF.
    • QGC_GST_USE_HOMEBREW (macOS, default OFF): builds against Homebrew's GStreamer instead of the official SDK; such builds cannot be installed or packaged (see macOS / Windows / iOS).
    • QGC_GST_CUDA_STUB_DIR (Linux): a directory with a cuda.h, such as gst-plugins-bad's gst-libs/gst/cuda/stub, for building the CUDA GPU path without the CUDA Toolkit.
    • GStreamer_REQUIRE_CHECKSUM and QGC_GST_DOWNLOAD_TIMEOUT (the SDK download's wall-clock cap, default 1200 s; its other limits are the shared QGC_DOWNLOAD_* ones) control downloads. GStreamer_REQUIRE_CHECKSUM=OFF lets an unpinned SDK version be checked against upstream's .sha256sum sidecar, or not at all; the Windows SDK installer still runs only when its checksum is pinned.
  • Plugin allowlist: gstreamer.plugins (common plus the platform's key) decides what is registered and bundled. Android links the requested plugins that have a static archive into libgstreamer_android.so; iOS registers only the requested plugins found in its merged archive, so the archive's other plugins (some GPL) stay out of the app; desktop installs bundle the allowlisted plugins found in the SDK and check after install that those and the bundle-required ones arrived. The plugin policy is gstreamer_plugin_policy in .github/build-config.json (bundle_required, optional_decoders, mock_server_debug, gpl_denylist, alternates, each described by the file's schema), outside the gstreamer section that keys CI's SDK caches; PluginPolicy.cmake reads it. Debug mobile builds add its mock_server_debug plugins (videotestsrc, mpegtsmux) for MockLink's test stream, which MockVideoStreamServer encodes with openh264enc (H.265 only where a desktop GStreamer has x265enc).
  • GPL: QGC ships LGPL GStreamer only. The policy's gpl_denylist names the GPL plugins and libraries of the official SDKs (x264, x265, a52dec, dvdread, ...); a desktop install that would ship one fails, and the macOS framework copy leaves them out. CI's artifact report also fails a package that ships one anywhere in it, or an Android or iOS binary with x264 or x265 linked in; the Linux AppImage only warns, a known issue: Ubuntu's libavcodec, which the bundled libav plugin loads, pulls in GPL libx264 and libx265. A missing bundle-required plugin fails a mobile configure. Configure warns once about allowlist names the SDK or system does not ship as plugins (misspelled, not built for the platform, or not installed) and once about shipped ones a static build cannot link; missing hardware-specific or alternative decoders (the policy's optional_decoders, e.g. qsv, va, libav, dav1d) only appear in a status line, as do optional components (RtspServer, Gl, ...) that were not found.
  • License notice: configure writes LGPL-2.1.txt and a NOTICE.txt naming the GStreamer version, its source code and, for an auto-downloaded SDK, the official packages and their SHA-256 (Notice.cmake). It also names the third-party libraries the shipped plugins bring (FFmpeg for libav, libvpx, dav1d, OpenH264 with a note on Cisco's patent license, and OpenSSL outside Linux) and, on iOS, which links GStreamer statically, how to relink the app with a modified GStreamer. They ship in share/licenses/gstreamer/ of desktop installs (macOS: QGroundControl.app/Contents/Resources/licenses/gstreamer/) and under licenses/gstreamer/ in the Android APK assets and the iOS app bundle. The dependency SBOM CI attaches to each package (generate_cpm_sbom.py) lists GStreamer from the same QGC_GST_RESOLVED_* cache entries.
  • CMake modules (cmake/GStreamer/): Orchestrator.cmake is the entry point this directory's CMakeLists.txt includes; its header lists inputs and outputs. platform/<platform>.cmake discovers each platform's SDK, and Acquire.cmake downloads its packages (Packages.cmake names them and their checksums) and extracts them with the platform's extractor in acquire/<platform>.cmake. Targets.cmake creates the targets the way Qt's FindGStreamer.cmake does, one pkg_check_modules(IMPORTED_TARGET) per library: GStreamer::Core, GStreamer::<Component> (Base, Video, Gl, RtspServer, ...) and GStreamer::GStreamer, which QGC links. The other modules are the helpers it includes, each described in its header. tests/ holds script tests and a fake-SDK configure fixture, run with ctest -L GStreamer.

Runtime Environment Setup

QGC configures GStreamer runtime paths before gst_init():

  • Desktop (Linux/macOS/Windows): GStreamer::Environment::prepareEnvironment() sets plugin paths, scanner/helper paths, and GIO module paths when bundled runtimes are detected (never in a Linux build with QGC_GST_BUNDLE_PLUGINS=OFF). A bundled runtime also gets its own plugin registry, gstreamer-<runtime version>-<arch>-<install hash>.bin in the app's cache directory (QStandardPaths::CacheLocation), unless GST_REGISTRY or GST_REGISTRY_1_0 is set, so it does not share the default per-user registry with the system GStreamer. The install hash keeps two installs of one GStreamer version (such as a stable and a daily build) from rescanning over each other's registry. It names what stays put across launches: the bundled plugin directory, the AppImage file ($APPIMAGE) rather than its mount point, which changes on every launch, and the app's name rather than the random directory macOS translocates a quarantined app to (such launches still rescan the plugins, whose recorded paths have moved). Each launch touches its registry and removes the gstreamer-*.bin registries there that no install has used for 30 days, such as those of an earlier GStreamer version or a replaced AppImage.
  • Linux AppImage: the same Linux bundle detection applies to the AppImage's usr/lib; it is skipped when the user already set a GST_PLUGIN_PATH* or GST_PLUGIN_SYSTEM_PATH* variable. deploy/linux/AppRun only sets the library, GIO and Qt environment.
  • Plugin search path: The bundled plugin directory is set as GST_PLUGIN_SYSTEM_PATH*, which replaces GStreamer's default system and user plugin directories. GST_PLUGIN_PATH* is cleared, because it would only rescan the same directory.
  • Plugin scanner: On desktop, GStreamer loads each new or changed plugin in its gst-plugin-scanner helper process, as it does by default, so a plugin that crashes while loading (a GPU driver probe, say) is blacklisted instead of taking QGC down on every launch. One scanner process loads every plugin of a scan, and GStreamer starts a new one only after such a crash. QGC points GST_PLUGIN_SCANNER* at the bundled scanner, and on Windows puts the app directory on PATH so the scanner finds the GStreamer DLLs. A missing, non-executable or failing scanner only logs a warning: GStreamer then loads the plugins in-process (the boot self-test fails on it). Android and iOS set GST_REGISTRY_FORK=no: their plugins are static, they ship no scanner, and iOS forbids fork().
  • TLS certificates: GStreamer's TLS users (RTSP over TLS, HTTPS sources) verify against GIO's default TLS database. On macOS and Windows, the bundled gioopenssl fills it from the system trust store (the Keychain's trust settings, the Windows ROOT and CA certificate stores), so roots an administrator or user added keep working; QGC leaves it alone and installs no CA bundle there. On Android and iOS, gioopenssl only knows OpenSSL's compiled-in certificate paths, which don't exist on the device, so the static plugin init makes the bundled ca-certificates.crt (CA_CERTIFICATES) the default database. On Linux, libgiognutls reads GnuTLS's system trust store.
  • Decoder ranks: QGC's decoder rank changes (the video decoder setting, the zero-copy preferences) skip the features named in GST_PLUGIN_FEATURE_RANK, which keep the user's rank.
  • Validation: A macOS app that ships GStreamer.framework without its plugin directory fails initialization with a clear error.
  • Plugin checks: After gst_init(), GStreamer::completeInit() checks the elements each feature needs, as Qt's qGstErrorMessageIfElementsNotAvailable does. Only the display core is required: the qgc sink elements, decodebin3, tee, queue, valve, capsfilter, a video converter, and the RTP/UDP source with its H.264/H.265 depayloaders and parsers. Without it, initialization fails: QGC says so in a message, and plays the streams through Qt Multimedia instead. A missing optional element logs one warning naming the feature it disables: RTSP (rtspsrc), MPEG-TS (tsdemux), TCP (tcpclientsrc), MKV recording (matroskamux) or MP4/MOV recording (mp4mux, qtmux). When any element is missing, a warning also names each plugin file GStreamer blacklisted, as one it could not load.

GPU bin selection

With gpu-zerocopy, qgcvideosinkbin picks one of four variants when it is built:

  • glupload (the default on Linux and Android): the converter feeds glupload → glcolorconvert → GLMemory. On EGL, gst-gl imports LINEAR and vendor-tiled DMABuf, at the cost of its own copy/convert; on GLX it uploads system memory.
  • dmabuf: DMABuf and DMA_DRM go straight to Qt's DMABuf importer, skipping gst-gl. It takes LINEAR and the plain tilings Qt's EGL display reports (see DMABuf zero-copy diagnostics).
  • d3d12convert: the converter feeds d3d12upload → d3d12convert → RGBA/BGRA D3D12Memory, for Qt Quick on Direct3D12 (QSG_RHI_BACKEND=d3d12), whatever QGC_GST_GPU_BIN says. It falls back to passthrough if the d3d12 plugin lacks those elements.
  • passthrough: the converter feeds the sink, which takes the GPU memory the renderer imports (see `gstqgc`). Windows on Direct3D11 and Apple builds use it.

glupload hands GLMemory to Qt through the GL share group, so it needs Qt Quick on OpenGL, over EGL or GLX. That holds for any OpenGL on Linux and Android, so the bin doesn't wait to learn which: it may be built before the scene graph runs, and Android can't tell EGL until then. dmabuf hands EGLImages to Qt's EGL display, so it needs OpenGL over EGL. Under any other renderer, such as Vulkan, the bin falls back to passthrough, which on Linux means DMA_DRM frames under Vulkan (see DMABuf under Vulkan) and system memory otherwise: gst-gl's GLMemory would otherwise be downloaded and uploaded again for every frame.

When both are compiled, QGC_GST_GPU_BIN (glupload or dmabuf, case-insensitive, read once per process) picks between them, and an explicit value is used even under a renderer the variant doesn't suit, with a warning. An unknown value logs a warning and keeps the default. Builds with only one variant ignore the variable: Android always uses glupload, Linux without gst-gl always imports DMABuf directly. The choice is logged once under Video.GStreamer.GstQgcGpuBin; the qgc GStreamer debug category logs each bin's variant.

Qt's xcb platform, which the AppImage uses even in Wayland sessions, picks GLX before EGL. Linux builds with the GL or DMABuf path therefore set QT_XCB_GL_INTEGRATION=xcb_egl at startup unless it is already set (HwBuffers::configureQtGraphics(), which the platform startup code calls through VideoBackend before Qt starts); Qt still falls back to GLX when EGL fails to initialize. Set QT_XCB_GL_INTEGRATION=xcb_glx to keep GLX, e.g. for a driver whose EGL misbehaves; video then reaches Qt as GLMemory that glupload uploads, without DMABuf import.

DMABuf zero-copy diagnostics

The direct DMABuf path hands each plane to Qt Multimedia's DMABuf importer as its own EGLImage, with the DMA_DRM modifier when the caps carry one. QGC adds what Qt's importer lacks: it waits for the decoder's writes first (on the GPU through the dma-buf's exported fence, else with a bounded poll()), and it keeps the buffer alive as long as the textures, and then until the GPU has finished drawing with them where EGL exports its fences (EGL_ANDROID_native_fence_sync): a V4L2 decoder doesn't wait for a buffer's readers before it decodes into it again.

  • It needs Qt to render with EGL: Wayland, eglfs, or xcb with QT_XCB_GL_INTEGRATION=xcb_egl. Under GLX, Qt's xcb default, the sink offers no DMABuf caps. Nor does it until Qt's EGLDisplay is known (QGCRhiCapture::qtEglDisplay: Qt's global share context, its platform integration, or the scene graph's OpenGL context); once it is, the sink asks upstream to renegotiate.
  • The DMA_DRM caps offer the (format, modifier) pairs Qt's EGL display reports when every plane can be sampled with that modifier. Plain vendor tilings (Intel X/Y/4, AMD without DCC) qualify; compressed layouts and those with auxiliary planes (Intel CCS, AMD DCC, Arm AFBC, Qualcomm UBWC) don't. Buffers negotiated with plain memory:DMABuf caps import without a modifier, leaving the layout to the driver.
  • EGL_BAD_MATCH (a broken EGL/driver pairing, QTBUG-112312), or 30 failed imports in a row of one layout (modifier, format and size, so that another stream's imports don't end the run), turn the path off until the scene graph is recreated: the sink stops offering DMABuf and DMA_DRM caps and asks upstream to renegotiate. Until upstream has, LINEAR frames take the CPU path. An import the driver refused counts, and so does any failed import of a tiled frame, which nothing else can show.
  • A tiled frame (a DMA_DRM modifier other than LINEAR) has no CPU layout: when no import takes it, it is dropped rather than shown scrambled, and counted in the sink's tiled-drops frame stat.
  • The Video.GStreamer.HwBuffers.GstDmaBuf and Video.GStreamer.HwBuffers.GstDmaDrmCaps debug categories log the offered modifiers and the first import; Qt's qt.multimedia.dmabuf category logs its EGL calls.

GstHwBuffersGlReadbackTest reads imported DMABuf planes back from a GL QRhi. CTest runs it on xcb with xcb_egl and skips it without an X display; a plain memfd stands in for a dma-buf under Mesa's software rasterizer, and a VA-API decoder's tiled output is compared with its system-memory output on real hardware.

DMABuf under Vulkan

Linux builds pin Qt Quick to OpenGL (HwBuffers::configureQtGraphics()); QSG_RHI_BACKEND=vulkan selects Vulkan instead. The DMABuf path then imports each plane of a frame as a single-plane VkImage on Qt's device, bound to the dma-buf through VK_EXT_external_memory_dma_buf with the frame's modifier (VK_EXT_image_drm_format_modifier), in the format Qt samples that plane as (R8, RG8, R16, RG16, RGBA8 or BGRA8). That is what Qt's EGL importer does with one EGLImage per plane, so NV12, P010 and I420 need no multi-planar image and no YCbCr sampler. Built with QGC_GST_ENABLE_DMABUF_VULKAN (on by default where Qt has Vulkan support and found the Vulkan headers); it needs neither gst-vulkan nor a link to the Vulkan loader.

  • Qt's device must have VK_KHR_external_memory_fd, VK_EXT_external_memory_dma_buf and VK_EXT_image_drm_format_modifier enabled, so QGC adds them to QT_VULKAN_DEVICE_EXTENSIONS before Qt starts; QRhi enables those the device has. When the scene graph starts, the device is asked once (probeDevice()) for the modifiers it imports each plane format with as a sampled image with one memory plane. It imports nothing when it lacks an extension or Vulkan 1.2: DMABuf frames then take the CPU path, and the Video.GStreamer.HwBuffers.GstDmaBufVulkan debug category says why.
  • The sink offers DMA_DRM caps only, for the (format, modifier) pairs every plane of which the device imports. Vulkan takes no implicit layout, so it offers no legacy memory:DMABuf caps; a dma-buf that arrives under plain video/x-raw caps, as a V4L2 decoder exports it, is imported as LINEAR.
  • Vulkan doesn't wait on a dma-buf's implicit fences, and QRhi's submission can't be handed the producer's fence, so the import first blocks, for at most 100 ms, until poll() reports the producer's writes complete, and takes the CPU path when it doesn't. The frame's buffer stays referenced until the GPU has finished the last frame that sampled its images, so the decoder can't write the next picture into it meanwhile.
  • The import gives QRhi the images in VK_IMAGE_LAYOUT_GENERAL, the layout that keeps a foreign producer's contents, without the queue-family transfer from VK_QUEUE_FAMILY_FOREIGN_EXT the specification describes: QRhi issues the barriers and has no hook for one.
  • Failed imports count towards the DMABuf path's disable latch as under OpenGL.

GstHwBuffersVulkanReadbackTest imports VA-API frames (a decoder's NV12, and I420, P010, RGBA and BGRA from the postprocessor, in whichever of the offered layouts the driver picks) into a Vulkan QRhi and compares QVideoFrame::toImage() with the system-memory frame; it also plays a VA stream into qgcvideosinkbin under a Vulkan render target. CTest runs it on xcb; it skips without a Vulkan device that imports dma-bufs, as Mesa's lavapipe doesn't. Verified on Intel (Mesa ANV with the iHD VA driver, LINEAR and Tile4 layouts) only.

macOS / Windows / iOS

GStreamer SDKs are auto-downloaded during CMake configure. To use a local installation instead, set GStreamer_ROOT_DIR (on iOS, a GStreamer.xcframework or GStreamer.framework, or a directory containing one). Configure fails when that SDK is older than the requested version (gstreamer.version.<platform> or QGC_GST_VERSION) or, on Windows, incomplete; an SDK Windows finds through GSTREAMER_1_0_ROOT_* or a standard install location is replaced by a download instead.

On macOS, configure uses, in this order: GStreamer_ROOT_DIR; Homebrew's GStreamer when QGC_GST_USE_HOMEBREW is ON; the SDK an earlier configure downloaded; /Library/Frameworks/GStreamer.framework; a new download. Homebrew's GStreamer (brew install gstreamer pkgconf) is also used when GStreamer_ROOT_DIR is a Homebrew prefix ($(brew --prefix)) or one of its opt/gstreamer or Cellar/gstreamer/<version> directories. Its pkg-config modules, and GLib's, are read with Homebrew's own pkg-config, which also finds the macOS system libraries' modules; gstreamer.version.macos (or QGC_GST_VERSION) is its minimum version. Such a build only runs from the build tree: Homebrew's libraries and plugins load each other by absolute paths under its prefix, so cmake --install and packaging fail with an error instead of bundling them.

Windows and macOS installs bundle the allowlisted plugins, the GIO modules and the helpers, and only the SDK libraries that those and QGC itself load, which install(RUNTIME_DEPENDENCY_SET) resolves at install time (libproxy's backends, which are loaded with dlopen, are installed as files too). A macOS GStreamer.framework is copied whole, without its GPL libraries.

On iOS only the GStreamerMobile framework links the SDK's merged static archive; GStreamer::GStreamer carries just its headers, defines and system-framework links. The app takes every GStreamer function from the framework, which exports those its registered plugins use plus QGC_GST_APP_CORE_SYMBOLS (Mobile.cmake). A new core call that no plugin uses therefore fails the app's link with an undefined-symbol error naming it: add the symbol to that list. The framework exports GStreamer's and GLib's API only (an exported-symbols list of name patterns, platform/IOS.cmake): the merged archive also holds FFmpeg, libpng, libjpeg and zlib, and the app's Qt code must link to Qt's own, which for FFmpeg are the frameworks qt_add_ios_ffmpeg_libraries() adds (cmake/platform/Apple.cmake). A post-build check (SingleCoreCheck.cmake) fails if the app still ends up defining core symbols. The plugin descriptors the configure reads from the merged archive (over 1 GB per GStreamer.xcframework slice) with nm are cached in the build directory, keyed on the archive's path, size and modification time. From 1.28.8 on, the iOS download is a tarball holding a GStreamer.xcframework instead of a .pkg, and only the xcframework's iOS slices are extracted, skipping its ~2 GB of tvOS slices.

Android

Auto-downloaded during CMake configure. No manual setup required. Each ABI build extracts only its own tree from the universal SDK tarball. Windows and macOS hosts query it with the pkg-config the SDK bundles for them (share/gst-android/ndk-build/tools/<host>/); Linux hosts use their own.

As on iOS, and as the SDK's own FindGStreamerMobile.cmake does, the app does not link the SDK's static archives: GStreamer::mobile is a shared libgstreamer_android.so (platform/Android.cmake) holding GStreamer, the allowlisted plugins, the OpenSSL GIO module and the generated gst_init_static_plugins(). It exports GStreamer's and GLib's API and hides the third-party libraries inside it (FFmpeg, OpenSSL, ...); QGC_GST_APP_CORE_SYMBOLS and the post-build SingleCoreCheck.cmake work as on iOS. The library links no Qt target, so on the 64-bit ABIs it sets the 16 KB segment alignment (-z max-page-size=16384) Qt gives Qt-linked targets for its android_16kb_pages feature: devices with 16 KB pages cannot load a library aligned for 4 KB, and Google Play rejects it. CI's android_apk_check.py checks every library in the APK for that alignment and for dependencies the APK or the NDK does not provide. Qt's deployment packages the library into the APK and loads it before the app. GStreamer's androidmedia plugin finds the Java VM and the app's class loader through gst_android_get_java_vm() and gst_android_get_application_class_loader() in that library, which return the JNI state src/Android/AndroidInit.cc hands it with qgc_gst_android_set_jni_state() at JNI_OnLoad and when the activity starts (Platform/gst_android_jni.c), so the SDK's GStreamer.java and GStreamer.init() are not used.

The plugins' Java sources and GStreamer's fonts and CA bundle are staged in the build directory (extra_java_sources/, extra_assets/), which android/build.gradle adds to the APK.

Windows users building for Android: Enable Developer Mode (Settings > System > For developers) to support symbolic links during the build.

Caching downloaded SDKs

Auto-downloaded SDK archives are cached to ${CPM_SOURCE_CACHE}/gstreamer-*. CPM_SOURCE_CACHE defaults to .cache/CPM in the source tree; set it to a location shared by your checkouts (e.g. ~/.cache/CPM) to download the archives only once (GStreamer 1.28.8: 0.3-1.2 GB per platform, e.g. ~1 GB for Android, 1.2 GB for iOS and 540 MB for Windows x64):

export CPM_SOURCE_CACHE=$HOME/.cache/CPM

CI caches the extracted trees under a key covering the gstreamer section of .github/build-config.json and cmake/GStreamer/{Packages,Acquire}.cmake, so keep everything that decides an extracted tree in those two files. Cached archives are verified on every hit against the checksum pinned in .github/build-config.json, or the upstream .sha256sum sidecar for an unpinned version; an auto-downloaded SDK is fetched again when its gstreamer.version.<platform> changes, and cached GStreamer probe results are recomputed whenever the SDK changes. Each archive is extracted once into a directory beside it that is stamped with the archive hashes, and a tree stamped with the pinned checksums is used without downloading or even keeping its archives (CI caches only the trees); configures sharing the cache take a lock instead of downloading or extracting over each other. The Windows SDK installer runs in its portable mode, so it leaves no GSTREAMER_1_0_ROOT_* environment variable, registry key or uninstall entry behind.

Supported protocols

GstSourceFactory accepts these URI schemes:

  • rtsp://, rtspt://: RTSP (the video stream only), over UDP and, when no UDP data arrives within 5 s, over TCP; rtspt:// over TCP only.
  • udp://: H.264 over RTP/UDP; udp265://: H.265 over RTP/UDP. Port 0 listens on a free port, and a URI without a host (udp://:5600) on every IPv4 address. A port that another listener holds fails to bind: udpsrc would share it (reuse), and of two listeners on a unicast address only one gets a stream's packets. Only the port of a multicast group is shared.
  • mpegts://: MPEG-TS over UDP. tsdemux adds its latency to that of a live stream, 700 ms by default: it is given the RTP jitter latency instead, and 100 ms at least, for the frame interval it holds each frame back by.
  • tcp://: MPEG-TS over TCP. tcpclientsrc connects inside the state change, on the receiver's worker, so it has a timeout of 3 s: without one it waits as long as the OS does for a host that doesn't answer.

Both MPEG-TS sources store the parsed caps on the stream object that tsdemux sends with the stream. decodebin3 picks the decoder by that object's caps when the stream starts, and stores the parsed caps on it only then, on another thread; tsdemux gives it those of the unparsed stream, which no decoder takes, and parsebin leaves them. On a busy machine decodebin3 could read them first and end the stream for want of a decoder.

Boot self-test

QGroundControl --simple-boot-test, which CI runs on every package (the installed Windows app, the macOS DMG, the Linux build tree and AppImage, the Android emulator and the iOS simulator), initializes video and then runs GStreamer::SelfTest::run() (GStreamerSelfTest.cc, through VideoBackend::selfTest()). The boot test fails when any of these checks fails, each logged as a critical message under Video.GStreamer.SelfTest that names the plugin, element or file:

  • Bundled plugins (a desktop runtime bundled with the app, as GStreamerEnvironment set it up; not a build tree using the system GStreamer, nor a QGC_GST_BUNDLE_PLUGINS=OFF build): no plugin in the bundle's plugin directory is blacklisted, as one whose library dependency is missing is; every plugin GStreamer registered from a file comes from that directory; and the registry and the directory hold the same plugin files.
  • Plugin scanner (bundled desktop runtimes): GStreamer did not log External plugin loader failed during init, i.e. the bundled gst-plugin-scanner ran. GStreamer for Windows tries the scanner before a scan and mentions one that doesn't start only in its debug log, so there the self-test starts the bundled scanner the same way: without arguments it must exit with 1 within 5 s.
  • Features: every feature in GStreamer.cc's plugin table has its elements. Unlike initialization, which needs only the display core, the self-test requires them all, since gstreamer.plugins.common and gstreamer_plugin_policy.bundle_required ship them on every platform.
  • GIO (bundled runtimes, Android and iOS): GIO's default TLS backend supports TLS and has a certificate database; where the bundle ships the libproxy GIO module, libproxy looks up the proxy for rtsp://127.0.0.1, which loads the backend libproxy opens by name.
  • Decoding: the 160x120 H.264 sample from SelfTest/Media/, embedded in the app, decodes through decodebin3 with the decoder ranks the app applied, to at least one raw frame; so does the 320x240 H.265 sample when an H.265 decoder is ranked for autoplugging (NVIDIA's HEVC decoders reject frames under 144 pixels high). When a hardware decoder fails mid-stream (as in a VM; decodebin3 replaces only one that fails to start), the check is retried once with software decoders forced, then the ranks are restored; a software decoder that works only logs a warning.
  • Recording: the H.264 sample, muxed by the recording branch's file sink into MP4 and MKV, makes a non-empty file that qtdemux or matroskademux reads back.

The whole self-test takes well under a second on a working runtime, gives each pipeline at most 4 s and stops within 15 s. It is quiet on success but for one summary line at info level, shown with --logging:Video.GStreamer.SelfTest.

Release builds forward up to the Debug level. The saved level and the forwarding are in place before gst_init() on the next launch, so what GStreamer logs while it initializes (the plugin scan) is forwarded too.
  • Native output: Setting GST_DEBUG, GST_DEBUG_FILE or GST_TRACERS keeps GStreamer's own log handler and turns the forwarding off. The log then goes to stderr, or to GST_DEBUG_FILE, in GStreamer's format, which its tools (gst-stats, tracer plotters) parse, in every build type. GST_DEBUG replaces the in-app level, and GST_DEBUG_NO_COLOR=1 drops the color codes. See https://gstreamer.freedesktop.org/documentation/gstreamer/running.html.
  • Command line: QGC's option parser rejects options it doesn't know, GStreamer's (--gst-debug-level, --gst-debug, ...) among them, so use the setting or the environment variables. GStreamer::initialize() does hand the arguments it is given to gst_init_check(), and keeps a threshold they set.
  • GLib messages (g_warning(), g_critical(), ...) always go to the Video.GStreamer.GStreamerLogging category.
  • The boot self-test logs its failures under Video.GStreamer.SelfTest, and its summary at info level.
  • Receiver flow (starts, stops, reconnects, the steps of an RTSP connection, the decoder chosen, pipeline latency, GPU path statistics): enable the Video.GStreamer.GstVideoReceiver category. The stream's health is also measured continuously: see Stream statistics. Failures are logged as warnings even with it off, with the failing element's debug string. The message the user gets ends with the cause taken from it: what the element said (bind failed: ...), the RTSP status of a refused request, or the plugin an element reported missing.

Pipeline Observability

QGC supports the standard GStreamer observability env vars. Set them before launching QGC. GST_DEBUG, GST_DEBUG_FILE and GST_TRACERS switch to GStreamer's native log output (see GStreamer Logging), so tracer records keep the format the GStreamer tools expect. The tracers below (latency, dots, leaks) are in GStreamer's coretracers plugin, which the plugin allowlist includes, so an installed QGC has them too.

Pipeline graph dumps (.dot files)

When GST_DEBUG_DUMP_DOT_DIR is set, QGC writes each receiver's pipeline graph there at key transitions as receiver-<stream>-<tag>.dot. <stream> is the receiver's name (videoContent, thermalVideo, or videoTile<N> for the N-th stream that got a tile, which keeps the name if the stream becomes the main picture), so the receivers don't overwrite each other's files, and <tag> names the transition (pipeline-initial, pipeline-started, pipeline-with-decoder, pipeline-with-videosink, pipeline-with-filesink, pipeline-error, pipeline-watchdog-timeout, pipeline-decoder-timeout, pipeline-recording-stopped, …). A transition's next dump overwrites its last one.

export GST_DEBUG_DUMP_DOT_DIR=/tmp/qgc-pipeline-dots
./QGroundControl
dot -Tpng /tmp/qgc-pipeline-dots/receiver-videoContent-pipeline-started.dot -o pipeline.png

When the env var is unset, QGC instead writes a snapshot to <CacheLocation>/qgc-pipeline-dot/receiver-<stream>-<tag>-<UTC timestamp>.dot on a pipeline ERROR and on a source or decoder watchdog timeout, keeping the 10 newest, so field-bug-report bundles include the topology automatically.

Every dump shows topology, caps, media types and states, but leaves out element properties, which can hold stream credentials (such as rtspsrc's user-id and user-pw).

Latency tracer

Per-element latency from source to sink:

GST_TRACERS="latency(flags=pipeline+element)" GST_DEBUG=GST_TRACER:7 GST_DEBUG_FILE=latency.log ./QGroundControl

Plot the resulting element-latency / latency log lines with any of the GStreamer community tools (e.g. gst-stats, GStreamerLatencyPlotter).

Live pipeline visualizer (gst-dots-viewer, GStreamer ≥ 1.26)

GST_TRACERS=dots ./QGroundControl &
gst-dots-viewer
# open http://localhost:3000

The viewer streams the live pipeline graph over WebSocket and exposes a snapshot button — no xdot viewer needed.

Leak / lifetime debugging

QGC_GST_DEINIT=1 GST_TRACERS=leaks GST_DEBUG=GST_TRACER:7 ./QGroundControl

Logs every GstObject / GstMiniObject that wasn't released by exit. Useful when triaging pad-leak regressions in the source factory or sink bin. The leaks tracer reports when GStreamer is deinitialized: QGC_GST_DEINIT=1 makes QGC call gst_deinit() as it exits, after its receivers are gone. It is opt-in, like Qt's QT_GSTREAMER_DEINIT, because GStreamer can't be used again in the process afterwards.
